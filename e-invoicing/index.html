E-invoicing means issuing and receiving invoices as structured electronic data instead of PDFs or paper. In the UAE, businesses exchange them through Accredited Service Providers approved by the Ministry of Finance.

A short history

  1. October 2024Legal amendments introduce e-invoicing concepts into UAE law.
  2. February 2026The Ministry of Finance publishes the first e-invoicing guidelines.
  3. 1 July 2026The pilot programme begins with a selected group of taxpayers.
  4. 1 October 2026Ministerial Decision No. 197 of 2026 takes effect. Transactions within the same VAT Tax Group are excluded for 24 months from 1 January 2027, the system failure notice is removed and the storage rule is relaxed. What changed.
  5. 26 October 2026The Ministry of Finance publishes version 2.0 of the e-invoicing guidelines. Who must comply.
  6. 30 October 2026Deadline for businesses with revenue of AED 50 million or more to appoint an ASP. The same day, Cabinet Decision No. 173 of 2026 replaces the e-invoicing penalties. The new penalties.
  7. 1 January 2027E-invoicing becomes mandatory for businesses with annual revenue of AED 50 million or more.
  8. 31 March 2027Deadline for businesses under AED 50 million and government entities to appoint an ASP.
  9. 1 July and 1 October 2027Mandatory for other in-scope businesses, and then for government entities.

Go-live dates are set by Ministerial Decision No. 244 of 2025. The Ministry of Finance can change them, so check its latest announcements before relying on a date.
