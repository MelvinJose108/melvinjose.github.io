"We're in a free zone, so we're fine." I hear it often. For tax, it doesn't settle much.

On 2 October 2026 the FTA published **Public Clarification TAXP010, Free Zones and Designated Zones for Tax Purposes**. Its point is simple: Corporate Tax, VAT and Excise Tax each define these zones differently, and **you must check your location against each law separately.**

### Four tests, not one

TAXP010 names four categories:

1. **Corporate Tax Free Zone**
2. **Corporate Tax Designated Zone**
3. **Excise Tax Designated Zone**
4. **VAT Designated Zone**

One business can be in a Corporate Tax Free Zone without being in a VAT Designated Zone. Or in a VAT Designated Zone without the location counting as a Designated Zone for Corporate Tax.

### 1. Corporate Tax Free Zone

A **Free Zone** for Corporate Tax is a defined geographic area in the UAE named in a **Cabinet Decision**, issued on the Minister's suggestion.

This is the starting point for the 0% regime. A **Qualifying Free Zone Person** must be incorporated, established or registered in a Free Zone, and operate from it, to get 0% on its Qualifying Income.

### 2. Corporate Tax Designated Zone

This is the one the clarification is really about. A **Designated Zone** for Corporate Tax is a zone that is a Designated Zone under the VAT Law **and** included as a Free Zone under the Corporate Tax Law.

> A VAT Designated Zone is **not automatically** a Designated Zone for Corporate Tax. It must pass both tests.

TAXP010 tells taxpayers to confirm with their **free zone authority** whether they are in a Free Zone or a Designated Zone for Corporate Tax.

### 3. Excise Tax Designated Zone

For Excise Tax, a Designated Zone is either:

- **a fenced free zone** that has security measures restricting the movement of people and excise goods in and out, is controlled and supervised by a customs department, and has an appointed **Warehouse Keeper**; or
- **any other area the FTA specifies**, provided it is a specific geographic area with the same kind of security measures and an appointed Warehouse Keeper. The Warehouse Keeper has to apply to the FTA to register the area.

To confirm, ask your **free zone authority**, or the **Warehouse Keeper** whether the FTA has approved the area.

### 4. VAT Designated Zone

A **VAT Designated Zone** is a fenced area listed in **Cabinet Decision No. 59 of 2017** (as amended). Being on that list is not the whole test: under **Article 51(1) of the VAT Executive Regulation**, it is only treated as outside the UAE for VAT if:

- it is a specific fenced area with security measures and customs controls over people and goods moving in and out,
- it has internal procedures for keeping, storing and processing goods, and
- its operator complies with the FTA's procedures.

So TAXP010 asks for **two checks**: find your zone on the FTA's published list, then confirm with the free zone authority that **the specific area where you're based** meets Article 51(1).

### Why distributors should care most

Under **Ministerial Decision No. 229 of 2025**, one of the Qualifying Activities for a Qualifying Free Zone Person is **distribution of goods or materials in or from a Designated Zone**. Being in a free zone isn't enough for this activity. The distribution itself has to run in or from a **Corporate Tax Designated Zone**, and it has to meet every condition:

![Conditions for distribution to be a Qualifying Activity for a Qualifying Free Zone Person]({{ '/assets/images/free-zones/dz-distribution.webp' | relative_url }})
{: width="1600" height="900" loading="lazy" decoding="async"}

- **What counts:** buying and selling goods, materials, component parts or other tangible or movable items. It can include importing, storage, inventory management, handling, transport and export.
- **Where:** in or from a Designated Zone, with goods entering the UAE imported through that zone.
- **Who buys:** a customer who resells, processes or alters the goods for sale or resale, or a public benefit entity.
- **Procedures:** any additional procedures the FTA prescribes, on top of every other Qualifying Free Zone Person condition.

The cost of getting it wrong is high. If a Qualifying Free Zone Person fails any condition at any point in a tax period, it **stops being a Qualifying Free Zone Person from the start of that period and for the next four**. That's five tax periods at the standard rates.

### What to do now

1. **List every entity and every physical location** your group operates from, including warehouses, not just the licence address.
2. **For each location, get written confirmation** from the free zone authority: is it a Corporate Tax Free Zone, a Corporate Tax Designated Zone, an Excise Designated Zone (or ask the Warehouse Keeper), and a VAT Designated Zone that meets Article 51(1)?
3. **If you claim 0% on distribution income,** check where the goods are actually imported, stored and shipped from, and who your customers are. Retail customers who consume the goods don't fit the definition.
4. **Keep the confirmations** with your Corporate Tax file. If the FTA questions your Qualifying Income, this is the first thing you'll need.
