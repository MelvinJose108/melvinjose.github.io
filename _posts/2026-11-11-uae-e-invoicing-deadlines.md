E-invoicing means issuing and receiving invoices as structured electronic data instead of PDFs or paper. In the UAE it is being introduced in phases by the Ministry of Finance (MoF), working with the Federal Tax Authority (FTA).

### How it works

- Businesses must appoint an Accredited Service Provider (ASP), an approved third party that sends and receives invoices on their behalf.
- Invoices are exchanged in a standard format (PINT AE) over the Peppol network, using a five-corner model in which the tax authority also receives the invoice data.
- Both the sender and the receiver connect through an ASP.

### Key dates

These were the dates reported at the time of writing. Confirm them with the MoF before acting.

- **1 July 2026:** a voluntary pilot phase begins.
- **Annual revenue of AED 50 million or more:** appoint an ASP by 30 October 2026 (extended from 31 July 2026), and go live from 1 January 2027.
- **Annual revenue below AED 50 million:** appoint an ASP by 31 March 2027, and go live from 1 July 2027.
- **Government entities:** appoint an ASP by 31 March 2027, and go live from 1 October 2027.

### What to do now

- Work out which group you fall into, based on annual revenue.
- Shortlist providers from the MoF list of approved service providers, and check each provider’s current accreditation status.
- Check that your accounting or ERP system can produce the required invoice data fields.
- Clean up customer and supplier details, such as legal names and tax registration numbers, because structured invoices depend on accurate data.

> Administrative penalties apply for missing the appointment and implementation deadlines, and the appointment date has already been changed once. Check the latest MoF and FTA announcements before you rely on any date here.
